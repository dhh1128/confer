## Executive summary

Overall posture is solid for confer's stated single-user-host trust model: the most security-sensitive surfaces (Discord inbound filtering, away-mode hook installer, IPC socket 0600 perms) were verified as correctly scoped, and the bulk of remaining items are LOW defense-in-depth or deferred hardening. Only one milestone-blocking item exists — OPS-F1 (HIGH, recommend-fix): the OIDC-privileged pypa/gh-action-pypi-publish step in publish.yml:37 is pinned to the mutable branch ref release/v1, meaning any push to that branch executes in a job holding id-token:write for PyPI. The most urgent action is to pin that publish action to a full commit SHA before the first release. Secondary recommend-fix items are softer: the publish workflow has no CI test gate on tag push (OPS-F2, MEDIUM), confer lacks a written threat model documenting the IPC trust boundary that the whole unauthenticated INJECT/LIST_ASKS design rests on (SEC-F1, MEDIUM), and ~534 lines of away-mode production code plus tests sit uncommitted in the working tree at risk of loss (OPS-F4, LOW but operationally fragile — worth committing promptly). The remaining LOW findings (socket grandparent dir, world-readable token warning-not-refusal, mutable tags on other publish actions, over-permissive top-level workflow permissions) are appropriately deferred or accepted given the single-user boundary.
